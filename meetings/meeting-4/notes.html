OpenAI says it reached AGI. Then it cancelled its next model.

2:03

"On September 3rd OpenAI put out GPT-6 Astra, its newest model. Someone asked OpenAI's president, Greg Brockman, if we've reached AGI. AGI just means AI that can do most things a person can. He said, 'I do think we're there.'"

"Then on September 28th, they cancelled the next version, GPT-6.1. Their own tests found it was more likely to lie than the one before it."

New York City paused student AI through 8th grade

2:10

"In September, New York City stopped kids from preschool through 8th grade from using AI at school for a full school year. That's about 600,000 kids."

"High schoolers can only use it in test programs, and only up to 50,000 of them. Teachers can still use AI to plan lessons. And chatbots built to act like your friend are banned for every grade."

"The parents who pushed for this say it doesn't go far enough."

DoPress once per card, then once for the last line. Source: NYC Mayor's Office press release, Sept 2, 2026. Some news sites reported ChatGPT and Claude blocked at every level; that isn't on the city's own page, so don't say it.

"What happens when you go the other way and hand students an AI tutor?"

8

Students offered an AI tutor got lower grades

2:12

"The University of Maryland ran a real experiment with 2,379 students. Some classes got an AI tutor built on ChatGPT and some didn't, picked at random."

"The classes with the tutor ended up about 4 points lower. Students who are the first in their family to go to college dropped more."

"Look at how people used it: almost all requests were 'explain this' or 'give me the answer.' Under 1% asked 'give me feedback on my work.' And only about 15% of the students even used it."

DoBe honest about the source: this comes from EdTech Innovation Hub (Oct 2), a small news site about school tech, and we couldn't find the paper yet. The researchers also say not to read too much into the first-in-family result.

"Now one about a school a lot of you are trying to get into."

"Meanwhile, a lot of colleges are giving up on AI checkers, the tools that guess if AI wrote something. Yale and Cornell tell professors not to treat them as proof."

12

AI is the top reason companies gave for layoffs this year

2:24

"Challenger, Gray and Christmas tracks every announced layoff in the US. Through September, companies said AI was the reason for 120,136 layoffs this year. That's 21% of all layoffs, more than any other reason."

"But in September alone, AI dropped to fifth place, about 9% of the month."

"But remember, a company picks what reason it announces. Saying 'AI' can sound better to investors than 'we're losing money.' Companies also plan to hire more this year than last year."

DoPress once for 5th place, once for the last line. Source: challengergray.com, Oct 1, 2026.

"So who's actually feeling it? Probably the people in this room."

13

New grads are struggling. Is AI the reason?

2:26

"Recent college grads, ages 22 to 27, are at 5.7% unemployment. Everyone else is at 4.1%. Normally new grads do better than average, so that's unusual."

"The case that it's AI: a Stanford study found jobs for 22 to 25 year olds in fields AI is good at, like coding and marketing, dropped 16% since late 2022. And almost half of recent grads say AI already changed hiring in their field."

"The case that it's not: a study of over 21,000 companies found the ones spending the most on AI actually have 12% more people in beginner jobs. And companies started hiring fewer beginners about six months before ChatGPT even came out."

DoPress once per side. Source: NPR, Aug 19, 2026. Other write-ups of the Stanford paper say 13% instead of 16%; if someone asks, say that.

"And what are the people running these companies saying?"

14

What the people in charge are saying

2:28

"In February, Microsoft's head of AI, Mustafa Suleyman, said most office tasks would be fully automated in 12 to 18 months."

"This month, he shared an estimate from Nobel-winning economist Daron Acemoglu: about 5% of work replaced over ten years. Acemoglu himself calls that a guesstimate."

"Then Monday.com, a work software company, laid off 20% of its workers in July, about 620 people, and told investors it expects more profit. They said it wasn't about replacing people with AI."

"Last thing: only about 11% of US workers are in a union, and right now unions are the main group getting AI limits written into contracts."

DoSources: Business Today (Oct 7), Globes (July), Axios (July 26).

"Your turn again. This one is personal."

19

Quick round: Power and water

2:43

"A data center is a huge building full of the computers that run AI. In Texas, 57% of voters say they don't want one near them. In September, the governor stopped approving new ones until the state checks it has enough power and water."

"Would you vote for a data center in Riverside? What would it take?"

DoPress T. Press next for scale: big tech plans to spend about $700 billion on data centers this year.

DoIf someone says it raises power bills: the group that watches one big power grid says data centers added $23 billion in power costs, and the data center industry says there's no proof regular households pay for it. Both sides say the numbers back them up.

"So who gets to make the rules for all this?"

DoPress T. Press next: the White House wants Congress to pass a law that cancels state AI laws like these. Nothing like that has passed yet.
