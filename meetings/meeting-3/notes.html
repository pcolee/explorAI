Part 2: Context

11

Context

2:25

“So that’s what it reads. Now: how much can it read at once?”

“Let’s look at everything that actually gets sent when you hit enter.”

12

Everything goes into one window

2:25–2:28

DoPress once per block, top to bottom.

“Every time you hit send, the app builds one big block of text: its own hidden instructions, any files you attached, the whole chat so far, then your new message.”

“Then it writes the reply one token at a time, and each new token goes back into the window before the next one.”

“That’s the only thing it can see. Even ‘memory’ features are just notes the app slips into this window.”

“So what happens when a chat gets longer than that?”

13

A tiny window, one chat

2:28–2:31

DoPress once per turn. Each chip is one message: ‘3 · 11’ means message 3, 11 tokens. The window is shrunk to 96 tokens so it fits on a slide.

“Turn 1, turn 2, turn 3: everything fits.”

“Turn 4 is 104 tokens and only 96 fit. So the app drops the oldest message, which is the one that said the quiz is Friday.”

DoPress for the pill.

“Then the student asks ‘what day is my quiz?’ and the model has to guess. It didn’t forget like a person forgets. It just can’t see it anymore.”

DoIf asked: real windows are huge, hundreds of thousands of tokens, and some apps summarize old messages instead. Same problem, it just shows up later.

“Which gives us the one rule for today.”

14

The rule

2:31

“So here’s the rule for today: it can’t read what you didn’t put in the window.”

“And if that’s the rule, the real skill is deciding what goes in.”

Five things that make an answer better

2:40–2:42

“Number one is the big one: paste the real material. The rubric, the syllabus, the job post. It’s the part people skip, and it matters most.”

23

Use the window on purpose

2:53–2:56

DoPress once per card.

“New topic, new chat: the old stuff rides along every turn and can steer the answer.”

“Paste, don’t describe. ‘My professor’s policy’ means nothing to it. The policy itself does.”

“Spell out the limits. ‘Under 100 words’ is something it can hit. ‘Short’ is a guess.”

“And give it a way out. Tell it to say so if the answer isn’t in what you pasted. You get fewer made-up answers.”

“If you forget everything else, keep this one sentence.”
