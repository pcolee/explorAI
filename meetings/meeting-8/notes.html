Use it, share it, publish it

2:08

"You built apps in Meeting 5. But most of them stayed in that one tab. Today I'll show you what to do once it works, so you can use it all finals week."

DoSwitch to the AI Studio tab with the finished grade calculator. Do each step live, then come back and press it in. 5 presses.

DoPress 1: use it. In the app, type in a few scores and change one. Point at the results changing.

"First, use it like a user would. Real numbers, every button. You're the tester. The AI can say something works when it doesn't."

DoPress 2: open aistudio.google.com/apps in a new tab and show the calculator in the list.

"Second, find it later. Every app you build shows up here, at aistudio.google.com/apps. Bookmark it."

DoPress 3: back in the app, show the sharing option (check where it is before the meeting).

"Third, share it. Your app is private until you share it. People you share with can use it and see its code, and what they use counts toward your limits."

DoPress 4: click Publish (top right), then Get started if it shows, Continue, Continue with free URL, and Publish your app. Open the link it gives you, ending in .ai.studio, and scan or type it on your phone.

"Fourth, publish it. Publish, then the free URL, and you get a real link. Anyone with it can open your app, on a phone too. Now it's on my phone for finals week."

DoPress 5: the sticky note.

"Free accounts can publish up to two apps, so pick your best. Use a personal Gmail: school accounts can't publish for free. Published means public, so don't put anything private in an app you publish."

DoOptional, if asked: the app's menu can also download the code as a ZIP or sync it with GitHub. If publishing fails live, share the app instead and show the build sheet's Keep it section. Source for the publish steps and the 2-app free tier: Google's "Deploying from Google AI Studio" docs (checked 2026-11-17). About 5 minutes.
