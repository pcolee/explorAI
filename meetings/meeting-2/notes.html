What AI is, generative AI, and how chatbots work

9

What is AI?

2:15

“Let’s start with the basics. You’ve probably been using AI for years without calling it that.”

10

Is it AI?

2:15

“I’m going to go through these one at a time. Hands up if you think it’s AI, hands down if you think it isn’t.”

DoAfter each vote, give the quick answer:

“Calculator: not AI. It just follows math rules someone programmed.”

“Spam filter: AI. Email services learned what spam looks like from millions of emails people marked as spam.”

“Autocorrect: AI today. Old versions just checked a dictionary, but modern keyboards learn from how people actually type.”

“Siri: AI. It came out in 2011, way before ChatGPT. It learned to understand speech from tons of voice recordings.”

“Google Translate: AI. It learned from millions of documents people had already translated.”

“Spotify recommendations: AI. It learns from your listening habits and from people with similar taste.”

“Face ID: AI. It learned what makes each face unique.”

“Google Maps traffic: AI. It predicts traffic from past patterns and live location data from phones on the road.”

“Thermostat: it depends. A basic one just follows a rule, like turning on the heat below 68 degrees. A smart one like Nest learns your schedule, so that one counts.”

11

AI learns from examples

2:19

“So what do the AI ones have in common? A calculator only does exactly what someone told it to do. AI learns patterns from lots of examples, which is why it can handle things nobody wrote an exact rule for, like recognizing your face in different lighting.”

12

Each one sits inside the last

2:20

“People use these words like they mean the same thing, but each one fits inside the one before it.”

“AI is the big umbrella. Deep Blue, the chess computer that beat the world champion in 1997, counts as AI even though it didn’t learn anything. It searched through moves using rules people wrote.”

“Machine learning is AI that learns from data, like spam filters. Deep learning uses big neural networks, like Face ID. Generative AI creates new things, like images or music. And chatbots are generative AI you can talk to.”

“So every chatbot is AI, but most AI isn’t a chatbot.”

13

Generative AI: the good and the bad

2:22

“At Club Rush, a few of you asked if this club is all about generative AI. It’s one part of what we’ll cover, not the whole thing.”

“A lot of people feel uneasy about it, and that makes sense. Deepfakes, scams, misinformation, cheating, and artists’ work being used without permission are real problems.”

“But it can also help you study, make things more accessible, and help you get unstuck. Our goal is to understand both sides so we can use it thoughtfully.”

14

The cat sat on the

2:24

“Finish this sentence for me: the cat sat on the…”

DoLet people call out answers.

“Most of you said “mat,” right? That’s basically what a chatbot does. It predicts the most likely next piece of text, adds it, and keeps going until the answer is done.”

“Those pieces are called tokens. A token is a chunk of text, sometimes a whole word and sometimes part of one.”

“Why does it predict? Because that’s how it was trained. It was shown billions of sentences with the next word hidden. It guessed, and it adjusted every time it was wrong. Writing a whole answer is just that guess repeated over and over.”

Myth or fact

15

“AI searches the internet every time you ask it something.”

2:26

“Myth or fact: AI searches the internet every time you ask it something. Hands up for fact, down for myth.”

DoClick to reveal.

“It’s a myth. Before a model is released, it’s trained on a huge snapshot of text: websites, books, articles, and code, collected up to a certain date. It learns patterns from that, but it doesn’t check the internet while it answers.”

“That’s why it might not know about something that happened last week. Some tools, like ChatGPT with search turned on, can look things up, but only when that feature is on.”

16

“AI understands what it’s saying.”

2:27

“Next one: AI understands what it’s saying.”

DoLet them vote, then click to reveal.

“Also a myth. Think of it like a really advanced autocomplete. It can describe heartbreak beautifully without ever feeling anything, and it can write a confident paragraph about something false because the sentences fit the pattern.”

“So don’t assume it knows what it’s talking about just because it sounds like it does.”

17

“AI can’t be biased. It’s just a computer.”

2:28

“AI can’t be biased, because it’s just a computer.”

DoVote, then reveal.

“Myth. It learns from data that people made, so it picks up our biases too. If most of its examples show doctors as men and nurses as women, it’ll tend to repeat that.”

18

“AI can be confidently wrong.”

2:29

“Last one: AI can be confidently wrong.”

DoVote, then reveal.

“This one’s a fact. It’ll say something false in the exact same confident tone as something true, and nothing warns you. That’s what we’re looking at next.”

Hallucinations and breaking AI on purpose

19

When AI gets it wrong

2:30

“When AI makes something up and presents it as fact, it’s called a hallucination. This is the biggest reason to never copy and paste AI answers without checking them.”

20

Two well-known hallucinations

2:30

“This has happened in real life. In 2023, two lawyers in New York used ChatGPT for research and turned in a legal brief citing court cases that didn’t exist. They even asked ChatGPT if the cases were real, and it said yes. The judge fined them $5,000.”

“That same year, Google’s first ad for its chatbot Bard said the James Webb telescope took the very first photo of a planet outside our solar system. That had actually happened back in 2004 with a different telescope, and Google’s stock value dropped by about $100 billion that day.”

“So even professionals and huge companies get caught by this.”

22

Sounding right isn’t the same as being right

2:34

“So why does this happen? It’s predicting what sounds right, not checking facts. A fake citation follows the same pattern as a real one, so it looks just as convincing.”

“And it’s trained to be helpful, so it tends to fill in gaps instead of saying “I don’t know.” Most of the time sounding right and being right line up, but not always. So double-check anything important.”

23

Break it on purpose

2:35–2:42

“Now it’s your turn. Pair up and try to make a chatbot slip up. There are some ideas on the screen. You’ve got about seven minutes, and then I’ll ask a few of you to share.”

DoWalk around and help pairs who are stuck. If someone asks why strawberry trips it up:

“It doesn’t see the word letter by letter. It sees chunks, like “str,” “aw,” and “berry.” So counting letters is like counting the r’s in a word you only heard out loud.”

DoNewer models often get strawberry right. If so, suggest a longer or made-up word. If someone asks about the math:

“It predicts numbers the same way it predicts words, instead of actually calculating, unless it has a calculator tool.”

24

Share your best fail

2:42–2:45

DoPick 2 or 3 pairs. After each one, give the quick why: letters and math break because of tokens and prediction, fake facts happen because it fills in gaps, and contradictions happen because it has no fixed beliefs.

What not to paste in

2:48

“Quick note on your data. What you type into a chatbot can be stored, and depending on the tool, it might be used to train future models.”

“So don’t paste in passwords, other people’s personal info, or sensitive details like ID numbers. A good rule: don’t type anything you wouldn’t be okay posting somewhere semi-public.”

“And every professor has their own AI policy, so when in doubt, just ask.”
