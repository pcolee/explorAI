How the terms fit together

Each one sits inside the last

Artificial intelligence · any system doing tasks we connect with human thinking · e.g. Deep Blue, the 1997 chess computer

Machine learning · learns patterns from data · e.g. spam filters, Spotify recommendations

Deep learning · machine learning with large neural networks · e.g. Face ID, Siri’s speech recognition

Generative AI · creates new text, images, or audio · e.g. image generators, AI music tools

Chatbots · generative AI you talk to · e.g. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini

Part 3

When AI gets it wrong

A hallucination is when AI makes something up and presents it as fact.

It’s happened in real life

Two well-known hallucinations

Lawyers cite fake cases (2023)

Two New York lawyers used ChatGPT for legal research and submitted a brief citing court cases that didn’t exist. A judge fined them $5,000.

Google’s Bard demo (2023)

In its first public ad, Bard said the James Webb telescope took the very first photo of a planet outside our solar system. It didn’t, and Google’s stock value dropped by about $100 billion.

Why it happens

Sounding right isn’t the same as being right

It predicts

It writes what sounds likely based on patterns. It isn’t looking anything up or checking facts.

Fake looks real

A made-up citation follows the same pattern as a real one, so it looks just as convincing.

It rarely says “I don’t know”

It was trained to be helpful, so it tends to fill gaps instead of admitting it’s unsure.

So always double-check anything important.
